How to share your clipboard between Windows and Android

You copied a link, an address, or a one-time code on your PC and need it on your phone, or the other way round. Emailing it to yourself works, but there are faster ways. Here are five that work today, with what each one needs and where it falls short.

By Tanishk Tewetia, developer of FerryClip · Last reviewed November 12, 2026

Disclosure: FerryClip is our app, so it is listed first. The other four are made by Microsoft, KDE and Google; each is a reasonable choice in the situations described below.

Quick answer

  • Any Android phone, no account, nothing leaves your Wi-Fi: use FerryClip or KDE Connect.
  • A recent Samsung, HONOR, OPPO, OnePlus or other supported phone, and you already use Phone Link: turn on Phone Link's cross-device copy and paste.
  • You already type with Microsoft SwiftKey and use a Microsoft account: turn on SwiftKey clipboard sync.
  • You only need to move text once in a while: Google Quick Share is enough.

1. FerryClip (free, open source, local Wi-Fi)

FerryClip is a small Windows tray app and Android app that pair with a six-digit code. Text you copy on Windows appears on the unlocked phone automatically. To send text the other way, copy it on Android and tap Send to PC in the Quick Settings panel or FerryClip's notification.

Clipboard traffic goes directly between your devices over TLS 1.3 with mutual authentication. There is no FerryClip server, cloud clipboard or account.

Set it up

  1. Download FerryClip for Windows (installer or portable ZIP) and the Android APK.
  2. Connect the PC and phone to the same Wi-Fi network or to the PC's hotspot.
  3. On Windows, open FerryClip from the system tray and choose Generate code to connect.
  4. On Android, open FerryClip and enter the six-digit code shown on the PC.
  5. Optional: add the Send to PC Quick Settings tile so sending from the phone is one tap.

Good for: any Android 10+ phone, privacy-sensitive text, offices or homes without a Microsoft or Google account on the PC.
Limits: plain text only; no iPhone; both devices must be on the same reachable network; the Windows build is not yet code-signed, so SmartScreen shows a warning on first install; the Android app is installed from an APK, not the Play Store. The setup guide covers each step.

2. Microsoft Phone Link: cross-device copy and paste

Phone Link is built into Windows 10 and 11 and pairs with the Link to Windows app on Android. Its cross-device copy and paste feature syncs text and images in both directions. According to Microsoft's support page, it works only on supported phones: Samsung devices on One UI 2.1 or later, Surface Duo, and selected HONOR, OPPO, OnePlus, Realme, ASUS, vivo and Xiaomi models.

  1. Open Phone Link on Windows and pair your phone if you have not already.
  2. Go to Settings → Features → Cross-device copy and paste and turn it on.
  3. Copy on one device, paste on the other.

Good for: supported phones where Phone Link is already set up.
Limits: phone support is limited, and it can use mobile data when you allow sync over mobile data. If your phone is not on the list, see alternatives to Phone Link clipboard sync.

3. Microsoft SwiftKey keyboard

If SwiftKey is your Android keyboard, it can sync clipboard text with Windows clipboard history through your Microsoft account.

  1. On Windows, open Settings → System → Clipboard, turn on Clipboard history and Sync across your devices, signed in with a Microsoft account.
  2. On Android, install SwiftKey, sign in with the same account and turn on clipboard sync in SwiftKey's clipboard settings.

Good for: people who already like SwiftKey.
Limits: you must use SwiftKey as your keyboard and sign in to a Microsoft account, and clipboard text syncs through Microsoft's cloud.

4. KDE Connect

KDE Connect is a free, open-source project from the KDE community that links a phone and a computer over the local network. It has a Windows version and does much more than the clipboard: file sharing, notifications, media controls and remote input.

Clipboard text from the PC reaches the phone automatically. Sending from a modern Android phone is manual: open the KDE Connect notification and tap Send clipboard, the same Android restriction explained below.

Good for: people who want a full phone-to-PC toolkit.
Limits: more features mean more permissions and settings to review if you only need the clipboard.

5. Google Quick Share (manual)

Quick Share for Windows can move text as well as files. To send from Windows, copy the text, open Quick Share and paste it; to send from Android, share the text to Quick Share and pick your PC. It is a send-it-once tool, not continuous sync.

Good for: occasional transfers when Quick Share is already installed.
Limits: several steps every time.

Why sending from Android takes one tap

Since Android 10, apps cannot read the clipboard in the background. Only the app you are using, or your default keyboard, can see what you copied. That is a privacy protection: it stops apps from silently collecting passwords and messages.

So apps that are not your keyboard, such as FerryClip and KDE Connect, ask you to tap a notification button or Quick Settings tile when you want to send. Keyboard-based tools like SwiftKey can sync automatically because they are the keyboard. The other direction, PC to phone, does not have this limit, which is why it can be automatic.

Troubleshooting local clipboard sync

  • Devices cannot see each other: confirm both are on the same network. Guest Wi-Fi and some office networks block device-to-device traffic (client isolation). Try the PC's mobile hotspot instead.
  • Windows firewall: if Windows marked your Wi-Fi as a Public network, local connections may be blocked. Switch the network to Private if you trust it.
  • VPN on either device: some VPNs send all traffic away from the local network. Allow LAN access or pause the VPN.
  • Phone stops receiving after a while: manufacturer battery savers can stop background apps. Exempt the app from battery optimization.
  • Text arrives but does not paste: Android may apply incoming text only after you unlock the phone.

FerryClip-specific steps are in the troubleshooting guide.

Frequently asked questions

Can I copy and paste between Windows and Android without an account?

Yes. FerryClip and KDE Connect both work over your local network without signing in to a Microsoft or Google account.

Is there a built-in way on Windows 11?

Phone Link's cross-device copy and paste is built in, but only for supported phones. Windows clipboard history can also sync to Android through SwiftKey with a Microsoft account.

Does clipboard sync work without internet?

Local-network tools such as FerryClip and KDE Connect only need the PC and phone to reach each other on the same Wi-Fi or hotspot. Cloud-based options such as SwiftKey need an internet connection.

Can I sync the clipboard with an iPhone?

Not with FerryClip, which supports Windows and Android only. Apple's Universal Clipboard works between Apple devices.

Is clipboard sync safe for passwords?

Prefer a method that keeps text on your own network and encrypts it in transit, and turn off saved clipboard history for sensitive items. FerryClip can skip recognized sensitive clips when saving history.
